Why we’re building DoorBangers

We wanted to
use it ourselves.

We built software to help people sell work around the home. The more we built, the more we wanted to put our own people at the door.

“Where’s the
actual work?”

Our SaaS customers kept asking us that question. We’d built tools for roofing and landscaping, including audio sales coaching and ways to show a homeowner what a project could look like. Quoting and follow-up connected to the CRM. We were excited about how much of the sales process we could help with.

The businesses using those tools needed customers with projects ready to go. As we talked with them, we started asking ourselves why we were stopping at selling software.

If we had confidence in the tools, we could use them to win the work ourselves. Selling a home improvement project would let us earn from the transaction and take responsibility for much more of what happened around it. We decided to build DoorBangers.

Someone who
knows the house.

We’re building a field sales force for the home, with AI helping each rep do the job. A DoorBanger will get to know homeowners and sell projects that fit what they need. Our operations team will match signed jobs with local providers who can deliver what was promised. We’ll track the work through completion and stay involved when a homeowner needs help getting a problem resolved.

A conversation that starts with roofing can turn toward the backyard. With visualization and quoting tools available during the visit, a rep can help the homeowner explore that project while they’re standing there together.

After a roof goes on, the rep will return to ask how it went and follow through on anything that needs attention. The homeowner deserves to be happy with the work they’ve already paid for. Over time, as other needs come up, that experience gives them a reason to call us again.

Over time, we intend to support a broad range of services around the home. Each one will need capable local partners and a process our reps can stand behind.

Better tools
in the field.

We expect AI to keep getting more capable and less expensive to use. We want those improvements in a rep’s hands as quickly as we can put them to work. Better coaching can help someone learn from a difficult conversation; a clearer visualization can help a homeowner explain what they want before anyone prices the job.

Our plans for the software extend into financing and assigning contractors, with fulfillment tracking keeping the team informed after a sale. We’ll judge those tools by what they help our people accomplish in the field.

Running the company also means hiring well, managing local teams, and taking responsibility when a job gets messy. As the technology improves, we’ll still have to earn a homeowner’s confidence in the person representing us.

What we intend
to prove.

We’re building DoorBangers to generate more gross profit per rep by taking good care of a household over years of working together. Offering several services gives a rep room to listen and help with the project that makes sense for that homeowner. Each job has to justify the money the customer spends and the trust they place in us. When the work goes well and we follow through, we earn the opportunity to help again.

A rep’s first year will involve a lot of prospecting. As that person builds a book of customers, repeat business and referrals will grow out of the care they put into each job. By their third or fourth year, our aim is for them to earn substantially more commission through relationships they’ve spent years earning.

We’ll measure gross profit per rep against the cost of delivering the work and supporting the team, with traditional single-product sales as a point of comparison. We’ll also ask homeowners whether the work lived up to what they were promised and track how problems get resolved. Cancellations and complaints will inform how we coach reps and choose providers. Repeat business has to be earned through an experience the customer is happy to recommend.

Room to build
it properly.

The capital will be used to recruit the first teams and give managers time to train them properly. We’ll build within each market, bringing dependable local providers on board and giving reps the support to stay involved through delivery. As more nearby households become customers, our teams will spend more of their time in neighborhoods where people know their work.

Funding will also give us room to choose our first services with the future customer relationship in mind. An affordable first job that solves a problem for the homeowner can give them a reason to trust us with a larger project later. We’ll choose those services for the value they deliver to the customer and the economics of doing the work properly.

Our ambition is a nationwide company whose reps have spent years getting to know the neighborhoods they serve. The first teams will give us evidence about what it takes to get there, including how much a rep can earn while doing work they’re proud to put their name on.
