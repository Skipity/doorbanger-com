Our approach

Earn the next
conversation.

When someone opens their door again because you took good care of them last time, you’ve earned something worth working for.

You’ll have to
prove yourself.

You’re asking a person to trust you with their home and their money. They have every reason to take their time. You might spend most of a conversation answering questions they’ve been carrying around since the last contractor let them down.

We want you to be good enough at this work that you can help them make a decision they feel good about months later. Getting there takes practice, and some of the most useful coaching will be about a conversation you thought went well.

Once the job is signed, your name is still attached to it. If a homeowner calls because the crew hasn’t arrived, helping them get an answer is part of your work. How you handle that afternoon will matter when they decide whether to introduce you to their neighbor.

And when they do introduce you, you’ll have to earn that person’s confidence for yourself.

A career that
builds on itself.

Early on, you’ll spend a lot of your day talking to people who have never heard of you. You’ll walk away from plenty of doors with nothing sold. Learning to stay thoughtful and interested through a day like that takes work.

We want those days to lead somewhere. A homeowner whose roof went on right has a reason to take your call. During a visit to check on the work, they might mention the backyard they’ve wanted to fix for years. You already know each other, and they’ve seen how you handle a job.

Over several years, we want a DoorBanger to build a book of customers who call them when something comes up around the house. You’ll still be meeting new people, with more of your time spent among people who know you.

That’s the career we’re working toward: experience that helps you earn more because you’ve become more useful to the people you serve. We’ll have to prove the economics as we build it. The expectation that you take care of people starts with your first customer.

When we do
our job well.

A homeowner has someone to call who remembers the house and will stay involved. That makes a difference when a project is expensive, unfamiliar, or going sideways.

For the local company doing the work, a well-sold job starts with a customer who understands what’s been agreed to. Our job includes getting those details right so the crew can deliver what the homeowner expects.

You earn commission for the business you bring in. Taking care of that customer gives you a chance to earn their next project, and DoorBangers grows alongside the relationships you build.

We’ll have decisions to make about which services to offer and which companies to work with. Being able to go back to the house, look the homeowner in the eye, and ask how it went belongs in those decisions.
