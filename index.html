01 / The testNo script for this part.

You like
people.

You like talking to people. More importantly, you like people. And you want to get even better with people.

You can walk up to a stranger’s house, make them comfortable, ask good questions, and truly listen to their answers.

You’ll work your ass off.

But you don’t need to turn into some hustle-culture lunatic to do it.

You keep your word.

You remember names.

You follow up.

You want to get exceptionally good at something hard. You take coaching without getting defensive.

You care whether the job actually went well after the paperwork is signed.

You want to make a lot of money.

But you’d rather tell somebody

“Your roof
is fine.”

than sell them a roof they don’t need.

And when you come back six months later, they open the door and say:

“Hey Jamie!
How’ve you been?”

Because they remember you. And remember you were good.

That’s a
DoorBanger.

02 / Know what you’re signing up for.

The
bad news.

This job is hard.

You’re gonna walk a lot. You’re gonna sweat. You’re gonna interrupt people who weren’t waiting around hoping a salesperson would knock on their door.

The
good news.

At first, everybody you meet is a stranger.

But for you, a stranger is just a friend you haven’t met yet.

Year one is hard.

But every time you take good care of somebody, something gets easier.

Imagine the conversations, a few years in.

The roof they call you about.

“Hey Jamie — roof’s leaking.

The backyard they ask you about.

“Do you know somebody

Become
